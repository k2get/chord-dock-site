0. Introduction

This page is a gentle guide to the chords shown in the Chord Dock chord palette. It explains each chord’s name, component tones, and rough sound impression in simple terms.

This is not a formal music theory textbook. It is written for people who are just starting out with music production. The goal is not strict theory, but to help you think, “ah, so this is roughly what this chord sounds like.”

Lite vs Extended

Lite: Covers the essential chords (triads, 7th chords, etc.).
Extended: Adds more colors such as 9ths, 6ths, and slash chords for richer harmony.

1. Basic Concepts

1-1. What is a Triad (3 notes)?

A Triad is a chord built from three notes. It is the most basic chord shape. In Chord Dock, many chords are based on a triad.

Example: C (C major)
Notes: C – E – G (3 notes)

1-2. What is a Tetrad (4 notes)?

A Tetrad is a 4‑note chord formed by adding one more note to a triad.
Chords written as “◯◯7” or “◯◯maj7” are basically tetrads.

Example: Cmaj7 (C major seventh)
Notes: C – E – G – B (4 notes)

2. LITE Edition: Available Chords Lite

This section explains the chords available in Chord Dock Lite.
Even with Lite only, you get enough chords to write a large portion of pop, rock, and many other genres.

2-1. Triads (3‑note chords)

Major

  • Example: C (C major)
  • Structure: Root, major 3rd, perfect 5th (R – 3 – 5)
  • Impression: Bright, stable

Minor

  • Example: Cm (C minor)
  • Structure: Root, minor 3rd, perfect 5th (R – ♭3 – 5)
  • Impression: A bit dark, mellow

Power Chord (5)

  • Example: C5
  • Structure: Root, perfect 5th (R – 5)
  • Impression: Strong and punchy, less clearly “major” or “minor”

Diminished (dim)

  • Example: Cdim
  • Structure: Root, minor 3rd, diminished 5th (R – ♭3 – ♭5)
  • Impression: Unstable, mysterious, tense

Augmented (aug)

  • Example: Caug
  • Structure: Root, major 3rd, augmented 5th (R – 3 – ♯5)
  • Impression: Floating, slightly strange

2-2. Suspended Chords

sus2

  • Example: Csus2
  • Structure: Root, major 2nd, perfect 5th (R – 2 – 5)
  • Impression: Open, ambiguous, fresh

sus4

  • Example: Csus4
  • Structure: Root, perfect 4th, perfect 5th (R – 4 – 5)
  • Impression: Feels like it “wants to resolve”

2-3. Tetrads (7th‑type chords)

Major Seventh (maj7)

  • Example: Cmaj7
  • Structure: R – 3 – 5 – 7
  • Impression: Jazzy, fresh, a bit sophisticated

Minor Seventh (m7)

  • Example: Cm7
  • Structure: R – ♭3 – 5 – ♭7
  • Impression: Bittersweet, relaxed

Dominant Seventh (7)

  • Example: C7
  • Structure: R – 3 – 5 – ♭7
  • Impression: Wants to move to the next chord, “bluesy”

Half‑diminished (m7♭5)

  • Example: Cm7♭5
  • Structure: R – ♭3 – ♭5 – ♭7
  • Impression: Unstable, a bit shadowy

Diminished Seventh (dim7)

  • Example: Cdim7
  • Structure: R – ♭3 – ♭5 – 𝄫7
  • Impression: Very unstable, maximum tension

Minor Major Seventh (mMaj7)

  • Example: CmMaj7
  • Structure: R – ♭3 – 5 – 7
  • Impression: Dramatic, a bit cinematic and mysterious

3. Extended Edition: Additional Chords Extended

In the Extended edition, you get chords that are not in Lite, such as extra “color tones” (tensions) and slash chords with different bass notes. Rather than heavy theory, we focus on what notes are added and how they feel.

3-1. Ninths (9th) and Add Ninths (add9)

What is a 9th?

A 9th is an extra note added to give a chord more color.
It is written with the number 9, as in C9 / Cmaj9 / Cm9, etc.

9th as a tension on tetrads (7th chords)

  • Example: Cmaj9 = Cmaj7 + 9th
  • Example: C9 = C7 + 9th
  • Example: Cm9 = Cm7 + 9th
  • Impression: Jazzy, deep, rich color

9th on a triad (add9)

  • Example: Cadd9 = C + 9th (D)
  • Example: Cm(add9) = Cm + 9th (D)
  • Note: These do not contain a 7th, so they’re a bit different from typical “tension chords”.
  • Impression: Bright and pop‑like; very common on acoustic guitar.

3-2. 6th Chords

  • Example: C6 = C + 6th (A)
  • Example: Cm6 = Cm + 6th (A)
  • Note: These also do not contain a 7th, so they feel closer to “extended triads”.
  • Impression: Gentle, slightly retro; common in bossa nova and jazz.

3-3. A Brief Note on 11th / 13th

Like 9ths, 11th and 13th are extra tones that add color to a chord.
In this document we skip detailed theory. When you see them in Chord Dock, it is enough to recognize them as “slightly more complex tension chords”.

3-4. Slash Chords (On‑Chords)

A slash chord is something like C/E, written with a slash.
The part on the left is the “chord”, and the part on the right is the “bass note (lowest note)”.

  • Example: C/E → chord C with bass E
  • Example: G/B → chord G with bass B
  • Impression: Makes bass lines smoother and can add a slightly bittersweet mood.

4. Notation Guide

This section summarizes the chord notation and symbols used in this document.

Basic Triad Notation

  • C: C major triad
  • Cm: C minor triad
  • Caug: C augmented triad
  • Cdim: C diminished triad

7th‑type Chords

  • Cmaj7: C major seventh
  • C7: C dominant seventh
  • Cm7: C minor seventh

9th / add9 / 6th

  • C9: C7 with an added 9th
  • Cmaj9: Cmaj7 with an added 9th
  • Cadd9: C triad with an added 9th
  • C6: C triad with an added 6th

Slash Notation

  • C/E: “C chord” with bass E
  • G/B: “G chord” with bass B
